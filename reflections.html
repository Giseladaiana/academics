Learning to Be My Own Mentor

Some of the most important lessons I have learned did not come from lectures, books, or examinations. They came from moments when I had to figure things out on my own.

Being your own mentor means learning to trust yourself, even when you are uncertain. It means asking better questions, being patient with your progress, and accepting that sometimes you simply do not know what the next step should be.

Over time, I have started to realise that independence is not about always knowing what to do. It is about learning how to find your way when you do not.

STUDENT ADVICE

How I Stay Focused (Most of the Time)

Staying focused is not always about having extraordinary discipline. For me, it is much more about creating systems that make it easier to return to my work.

There are days when motivation is high and studying feels almost effortless. But there are also days when everything feels difficult, deadlines are approaching, and even opening a notebook feels like too much.

What has helped me most is accepting that consistency does not mean performing perfectly every day.

Dealing with Doubt and Comparison

It is surprisingly easy to look around and feel that everyone else knows exactly what they are doing.

Someone seems to understand the material faster. Someone else has already found their research topic, their career path, or their next opportunity.

But comparison rarely shows us the full picture. We see other people's progress without seeing their uncertainty, their mistakes, or the difficult moments that came before it.
